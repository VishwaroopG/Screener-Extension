Dividend stocks in India

High dividend stocks in India - Coal India, ONGC, Power Grid, ITC and more - dividend yield screens, payout traps and FAQs.

IndiaDividendsPSUIncome

India's highest sustainable dividends mostly come from two wells: cash-cow PSUs (coal, oil, power-grid monopolies that the government nudges to pay out) and mature blue-chips (ITC, HCL Tech) converting steady cash flows into rising payouts.

Yield alone misleads - a 9% yield on a falling stock is a trap, not income. Screen for payout ratio, dividend growth streak and free-cash-flow cover, and remember dividends are now taxable in the shareholder's hands.

How to screen dividend stocks yourself

  • Yield between 2% and 7% - above that, suspect a price collapse rather than generosity.
  • Payout ratio under 80% - leaves room to sustain dividends when earnings dip a year.
  • Dividend growth over 5 years - growers beat static high-yielders on total return.
  • Free-cash-flow cover above 1.2x - dividends paid from borrowings are a countdown, not income.

Risks to watch

  • One-off special dividends. Asset-sale payouts inflate trailing yield and vanish next year.
  • Ignoring dividend taxation. Since dividends are taxed as income, compare post-tax yield with debt options.
  • Yield-chasing into cyclicals at peak. Commodity dividends look fattest exactly when the cycle is about to turn.

Frequently asked questions

Which Indian stock pays the highest dividend?

It rotates yearly among PSU cash cows like Coal India, ONGC and oil marketers. Rank by sustainable yield - payout ratio plus cash cover - not last year's cheque. Learn dividend yield first.

Are dividends better than growth?

Different jobs: dividends suit income needs and volatile markets; growth suits long horizons. Total return (price + dividends) is the only fair scoreboard.

Do I pay tax on dividends in India?

Yes - dividends are taxable in your hands at your slab rate, with TDS above thresholds. Factor this into yield math.

Dividend Yield

Annual dividend divided by share price. A 4% yield pays Rs 4 yearly per Rs 100 invested - if the payout lasts.

P/E Ratio

Price-to-earnings: what you pay for each rupee or dollar of yearly profit. The starting lens for cheap vs expensive.

ROE

Return on equity: profit earned per rupee of shareholder money. High and stable ROE compounds wealth.

Market Cap

Price times shares outstanding: the market's price tag for the whole company. Defines large, mid and small caps.
