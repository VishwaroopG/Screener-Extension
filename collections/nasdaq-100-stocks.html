Nasdaq 100 stocks

Nasdaq 100 stocks - top holdings, tech concentration, QQQ tracking, how it differs from the S&P 500, and live tracking tips.

USIndexNasdaqTech

The Nasdaq 100 holds 100 large non-financial companies listed on Nasdaq - in practice, America's technology growth index. Apple, Microsoft and Nvidia alone decide a large share of its daily move, which is why it outruns the S&P 500 in rallies and underperforms in sell-offs.

Track it through QQQ (the mirroring ETF) plus a watchlist of its top weights: when mega-cap tech earnings land, this index tells you the market's verdict within minutes.

How to screen Nasdaq 100 yourself

  • Mega-cap earnings calendar - six reporting days a year decide most of the index's annual move; never be surprised by them.
  • Growth-vs-multiple check - the index trades well above the S&P 500's P/E; confirm earnings growth justifies it each quarter.
  • Non-tech participation - Costco, PepsiCo and Amgen holding up means breadth; tech-only rallies are fragile.
  • QQQ premium/discount and volumes - the ETF should track tightly; persistent gaps signal stress.

Risks to watch

  • Assuming Nasdaq means all-tech. Consumer (Costco, PepsiCo), healthcare (Amgen) and others dilute purity - check actual weights.
  • Leveraged QQQ products as holdings. TQQQ-style daily leverage decays; trade them or skip them.
  • Forgetting rate sensitivity. Long-duration growth valuations fall fastest when yields spike.

Frequently asked questions

Nasdaq vs Nasdaq 100 - what's the difference?

The Nasdaq Composite holds 3,000+ stocks; the Nasdaq 100 holds the top 100 non-financials. QQQ tracks the 100 - that is the tradable one.

Why is the Nasdaq 100 so volatile?

Concentration plus growth multiples: a handful of mega-caps with 25–35x earnings swing harder than a broad market on every rate or earnings headline.

S&P 500 vs Nasdaq 100 - which should beginners pick?

The S&P 500 for core breadth, Nasdaq 100 as a growth satellite. Our index guide compares all three US benchmarks.
