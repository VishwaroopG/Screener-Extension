Bank stocks in India

Banks are the economy in stock form: credit growth tracks nominal GDP, and asset quality tracks the cycle. India's pack divides into private leaders (HDFC Bank, ICICI, Kotak, Axis) prized for CASA deposits and tech, and PSU giants (SBI, Bank of Baroda) prized for reach and improving balance sheets.

Bank analysis has its own vocabulary — CASA, NIM, GNPA, PCR, ROA — and P/E means little without them. The screen below is the five-number health check professionals run first.

How to screen bank stocks yourself

  • CASA above 40% — cheap deposits are the moat; falling CASA foreshadows margin pressure.
  • NIM defended above 3% (private) / 2.5% (PSU) — margins, not loan growth, decide returns.
  • GNPA under 2% with PCR above 70% — clean books first, growth second.
  • ROA above 1% — the line between an average bank and a compounding one.
  • Credit-deposit ratio under 85% — headroom to grow without expensive bulk deposits.

Risks to watch

  • Valuing banks on P/E alone. Use price-to-book adjusted for asset quality — a cheap P/E often prices hidden NPAs.
  • Unsecured-lending euphoria. Personal-loan booms flatter growth until the cycle turns; watch RBI risk-weight signals.
  • Ignoring deposit wars. When every bank chases deposits, margins compress across the board.

Frequently asked questions

HDFC Bank vs ICICI Bank — which is better?

Both are quality franchises; compare CASA, NIM trajectory and ROA rather than brand. ICICI has led on returns recently while HDFC digests its merger — the numbers, not narratives, decide.

Are PSU bank stocks good investments?

They have rerated on cleaner books and record dividends, but trade cheaper for a reason: government ownership caps. They suit value-style allocations, not buy-and-forget compounding.

What is CASA and why does it matter?

Current and savings account deposits — the cheapest funding a bank gets. Higher CASA means lower costs and fatter margins. Defined in our CASA glossary.

P/E Ratio

Price-to-earnings: what you pay for each rupee or dollar of yearly profit. The starting lens for cheap vs expensive.

ROE

Return on equity: profit earned per rupee of shareholder money. High and stable ROE compounds wealth.

Market Cap

Price times shares outstanding: the market's price tag for the whole company. Defines large, mid and small caps.

Dividend Yield

Annual dividend divided by share price. A 4% yield pays Rs 4 yearly per Rs 100 invested — if the payout lasts.
