IT stocks in India

IT stocks in India — TCS, Infosys, HCL Tech, Persistent and more — deal wins, attrition and margin screens, US-recession risks, FAQs.

IndiaITNifty ITUS Exposure

Indian IT earns mostly in dollars and spends mostly in rupees — so every stock here is a joint bet on US corporate tech budgets and the USD-INR rate. Tier-1 giants (TCS, Infosys, HCL Tech, Wipro, Tech Mahindra) offer stability and dividends; mid-caps (Persistent, Coforge) offer growth at richer multiples.

The cycle turns on three data points: large-deal TCV, BFSI commentary and attrition. Screen on those plus margins, and never buy IT for dividend yield alone when growth stalls.

How to screen IT stocks yourself

  • Large-deal TCV growing year-on-year — the forward order book of IT services.
  • EBIT margin defended (tier-1: 20%+, mid-cap: 14%+) — pricing power shows up here first.
  • Attrition stabilising under 15% — spiking attrition means wage pressure and delivery risk.
  • BFSI commentary improving — banks are the biggest spenders; their budgets lead sector revenue by quarters.
  • Free-cash-flow conversion near 100% — IT should convert profits to cash; if not, ask why.

Risks to watch

  • Buying US-recession dips too early. IT underperforms for quarters after US slowdown signals — wait for deal-win confirmation.
  • Paying 50x for mid-cap growth that slows. Mid-cap IT premiums evaporate the quarter growth converges to tier-1 levels.
  • Ignoring currency. A sharp rupee rally can erase a quarter's operational gains.

Frequently asked questions

Why do IT stocks fall when US markets fall?

Roughly 60%+ of revenue comes from the US. Recession fears mean budget cuts, delayed deals and pricing pressure — the stocks price that chain instantly.

TCS vs Infosys — which is better?

TCS for consistency and buybacks, Infosys for large-deal momentum. Compare TCV, margin trajectory and capital return rather than stock tips.

Are IT stocks good for dividends?

Tier-1 IT pays generously (HCL Tech, TCS, Infosys all return cash), but buy them for total return — dividends alone cannot offset a growth downgrade.

ROE
