How to screen S&P 500 yourself

  • Sector weights, not stock tips - know tech is ~30%+ before blaming 'the market' for a Nasdaq-led fall.
  • Equal-weight (RSP) vs cap-weight (SPY) - breadth lives in the equal-weight version; narrow rallies show up as divergence.
  • Earnings season participation - 70%+ beating estimates is normal; guidance, not beats, moves prices.
  • Advance/decline inside the 500 - when the index rises on 150 winners, the rally is fragile.

Risks to watch

  • Assuming 500 stocks means diversified. Cap-weighting means ten stocks decide your fate.
  • Timing the index on headlines. Time in beats timing - the 500's long-run return comes from compounding, not trading.
  • Ignoring currency as an Indian investor. Your S&P 500 return in rupees includes the USD-INR move.

Frequently asked questions

How many stocks are in the S&P 500?

500 large-cap US companies (technically 503 securities, since some firms have two share classes). Membership is decided by an S&P committee on size, liquidity and profitability.

S&P 500 vs Nasdaq 100 - which is better?

The S&P 500 is broader across sectors; the Nasdaq 100 is tech-concentrated and historically faster-growing but more volatile. Many investors hold both. Compare in our index guide.

Can Indians invest in the S&P 500?

Yes - via Indian feeder mutual funds/ETFs or direct US-stock platforms under the LRS limit. Check expense ratios, tracking error and tax rules before choosing a route.
