Magnificent 7 US stocks

Magnificent 7 stocks - Apple, Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Tesla - weights, AI exposure, concentration risks and FAQs.

USBig TechAIS&P 500

Seven companies - Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Tesla - drive roughly a third of the S&P 500's value. They are called the Magnificent 7 because market returns in recent years have depended disproportionately on them.

Owning the Mag 7 means owning the AI capex cycle, cloud growth and digital advertising in one basket - plus concentration risk no diversified investor should ignore. Track them as a group: when they move together, the whole index follows.

How to screen Magnificent 7 yourself

  • Revenue growth vs multiple - 30x+ earnings needs 15%+ sustained growth; check PEG, not just P/E.
  • Free-cash-flow after AI capex - record spending is fine only if cash generation keeps up.
  • Cloud/ad growth reacceleration - AWS, Azure, GCP and ad prints lead Mag 7 earnings reactions.
  • Weight in your own portfolio - index funds already give you ~30% Mag 7; adding more doubles down knowingly or not.

Risks to watch

  • Treating all seven as identical. Nvidia is cyclical semis, Apple is consumer hardware, Tesla is autos-plus-options - different risks, different cycles.
  • Ignoring concentration. When the Mag 7 sneeze, the S&P 500 catches cold - diversify deliberately, not accidentally.
  • Chasing earnings gaps. These stocks routinely swing 5–10% post-earnings; position before, don't chase after.

Frequently asked questions

What are the Magnificent 7 stocks?

Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Tesla - the seven mega-caps dominating US index weights and returns. The label stuck around 2023 as AI spending concentrated gains.

Should I buy all 7 or an index fund?

An S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100 fund already holds all seven at market weight - stock-picking here is an overweight bet, so size it as one.

Why is Tesla in the Mag 7 with lower earnings?

Inclusion reflects market-cap dominance and narrative weight (EVs, autonomy, AI), not earnings parity. It is the most volatile of the seven - see market cap vs earnings.
