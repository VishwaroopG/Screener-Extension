Expense Ratio

A fund's yearly fee as % of assets. A 1% fee silently eats a quarter of long-run gains - minimise it.

The expense ratio is what ETFs and mutual funds charge yearly. Active equity funds take ~1–2%, index funds under ~0.3% - the gap compounds brutally over decades.

On a 12% gross return over 20 years, 2% fees versus 0.2% fees is the difference between ~9.6x and ~13.6x your money. Fees are the most reliable predictor of fund underperformance.

Example

Two identical Nifty index funds at 0.05% and 0.30% fees diverge by lakhs on a crore-sized SIP over 15 years - for zero extra service.

Frequently asked questions

Does expense ratio include exit load?

No - exit loads and taxes are separate. Total cost = expense ratio drag + loads + your tax on gains.

Related terms

ETF

Exchange-traded fund: a basket of stocks, bonds or gold bought like one share. Instant diversification, tiny fees.

Index

A rule-based basket measuring a market slice - Nifty 50, S&P 500, gold prices. The benchmark everything gets judged against.

IPO

Initial public offering: a private company selling shares to the public first. Hype is high, history is short - screen harder.
