Index

A rule-based basket measuring a market slice — Nifty 50, S&P 500, gold prices. The benchmark everything gets judged against.

An index tracks a defined basket by fixed rules: membership criteria, weighting (usually market-cap) and review schedules. It turns 'the market' from a feeling into a number.

Indices power ETFs and index funds, benchmark active managers, and settle derivatives. Know an index's construction before concluding anything from its level.

Example

Judging your 12% return? Against a Nifty at +8% you won; against Nasdaq-100 at +25% you chose the wrong arena. Benchmarks make returns honest.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy an index directly?

No — buy its ETF or index fund shadow. Compare expense ratio and tracking error between shadows of the same index.

Related terms

ETF

Exchange-traded fund: a basket of stocks, bonds or gold bought like one share. Instant diversification, tiny fees.

Nifty 50

India's benchmark: 50 large stocks across 14+ sectors on the NSE. When India 'is up 1%', this is usually the number.

S&P 500

500 large US companies, cap-weighted. The default scoreboard for American equities — and your global benchmark.
