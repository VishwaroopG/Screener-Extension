CASA

Current + savings account deposits - a bank's cheapest funding. Higher CASA means fatter margins and a stronger moat.

CASA (current account + savings account) deposits cost banks ~3% versus ~7% for term deposits. A 45% CASA ratio structurally lowers funding cost and defends margins when rates rise.

Falling CASA industry-wide signals deposit wars: every bank pays more for funds, and net interest margins compress across the board.

Example

Two banks with identical loan books: the 45%-CASA one earns ~0.5% extra margin yearly - compounding into a valuation gap within three years.

Frequently asked questions

What CASA is good?

Above 40% is strong for large banks; SBI-scale CASA above 42% is exceptional. Trend matters more than a single quarter.

Related terms

ROE

Return on equity: profit earned per rupee of shareholder money. High and stable ROE compounds wealth.

P/B Ratio

Price-to-book: market price versus net asset value per share. The classic yardstick for banks and asset-heavy firms.

Dividend Yield

Annual dividend divided by share price. A 4% yield pays Rs 4 yearly per Rs 100 invested - if the payout lasts.
