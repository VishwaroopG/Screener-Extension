P/E Ratio

Price-to-earnings: what you pay for each rupee or dollar of yearly profit. The starting lens for cheap vs expensive.

The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio divides a stock's price by its earnings per share over the last twelve months. A P/E of 20 means investors pay Rs 20 (or $20) for every Rs 1 ($1) of annual profit.

Rough landmarks: under ~15 is value territory, 15–25 is the broad-market norm, above ~30 prices in serious future growth. Always compare within a sector - a utility at 12x and software at 35x can both be fairly priced.

Example

A stock at Rs 500 earning Rs 25 per share trades at 20x. If earnings double to Rs 50 and the price stays put, the P/E halves to 10x - the classic value-setup arithmetic.

Frequently asked questions

Is a low P/E always a buy?

No - it can signal a bargain or a business in decline. Check earnings trend, debt and peers first.

Why do loss-making companies show no P/E?

Negative or zero earnings make the ratio meaningless. Use price-to-sales or cash-flow multiples instead.

Related terms

ROCE

Return on capital employed: how efficiently a company turns invested money into profit. Above 15–20% signals quality.

EPS

Earnings per share: the company's yearly profit divided by share count. The 'E' inside P/E.

Market Cap

Price times shares outstanding: the market's price tag for the whole company. Defines large, mid and small caps.
