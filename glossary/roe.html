ROE

Return on equity: profit earned per rupee of shareholder money. High and stable ROE compounds wealth.

ROE (return on equity) divides net profit by shareholder equity. A 20% ROE means each Rs 100 of shareholder funds generated Rs 20 of profit this year.

Read it with leverage in mind: heavy borrowing shrinks equity and flatters ROE. Pair it with ROCE and debt-to-equity before concluding a company is excellent.

Example

A bank earning 1.2% on assets (ROA) with 8x leverage posts roughly 15%+ ROE - normal for banking, alarming anywhere else. Context decides.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good ROE?

Above 15% sustained over 5+ years is strong in most sectors; banks run higher on leverage, utilities lower on asset intensity. Trend beats level.

Related terms

ROCE

Return on capital employed: how efficiently a company turns invested money into profit. Above 15–20% signals quality.

P/E Ratio

Price-to-earnings: what you pay for each rupee or dollar of yearly profit. The starting lens for cheap vs expensive.

P/B Ratio

Price-to-book: market price versus net asset value per share. The classic yardstick for banks and asset-heavy firms.
