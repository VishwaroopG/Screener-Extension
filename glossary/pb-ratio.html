P/B Ratio

Price-to-book: market price versus net asset value per share. The classic yardstick for banks and asset-heavy firms.

The price-to-book (P/B) ratio compares market price to book value per share (assets minus liabilities). A P/B of 2 means investors pay twice the accounting net worth.

It works best where book value is meaningful - banks, insurers, utilities. It misleads for asset-light software or brand businesses whose real value never sits on the balance sheet.

Example

A bank trading at 1.2x book with 1%+ ROA is usually cheaper than a rival at 0.8x book hiding bad loans - asset quality adjusts every P/B reading.

Frequently asked questions

Is P/B under 1 always cheap?

Often it prices distress - bad loans, stranded assets or structural decline. Cheap on book still needs clean assets underneath.

Related terms

ROE

Return on equity: profit earned per rupee of shareholder money. High and stable ROE compounds wealth.

P/E Ratio

Price-to-earnings: what you pay for each rupee or dollar of yearly profit. The starting lens for cheap vs expensive.

Market Cap

Price times shares outstanding: the market's price tag for the whole company. Defines large, mid and small caps.
