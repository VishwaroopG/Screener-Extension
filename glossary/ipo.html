IPO

Initial public offering: a private company selling shares to the public first. Hype is high, history is short — screen harder.

An IPO lists a company publicly via a price band, subscription window and allotment. Oversubscription makes headlines; fundamentals make returns.

New listings lack price history, so screen the prospectus: use of proceeds, promoter holding, anchor investors, and whether private-equity sellers are exiting at your entry price.

Example

IPOs subscribed 100x+ on retail frenzy routinely list flat and drift for a year — demand for the paper exceeded demand for the business.

In Ticker Screener

Add IPO allotments to a dedicated portfolio the listing morning; price alerts catch the post-listing base forming while hype fades.

Frequently asked questions

Are IPOs good investments?

Selectively. Established-profit IPOs at sane multiples compound; loss-making story IPOs at peak multiples mostly transfer wealth from public to promoters.

Related terms

Market Cap

Price times shares outstanding: the market's price tag for the whole company. Defines large, mid and small caps.

EPS

Earnings per share: the company's yearly profit divided by share count. The 'E' inside P/E.

P/E Ratio

Price-to-earnings: what you pay for each rupee or dollar of yearly profit. The starting lens for cheap vs expensive.
