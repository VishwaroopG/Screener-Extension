ETF vs stock: which should you pick?

When index ETFs beat stock-picking (core holdings) and when single stocks win (conviction) - plus the fee math that decides.

An ETF buys the whole basket: diversification, sub-0.3% fees and zero research burden. A stock buys one thesis: concentration, upside and the risk of being wrong.

Rule of thumb: core in ETFs, satellite in stocks. Index funds for the Nifty 500 / S&P 500 spine of your portfolio; 5–15 hand-picked stocks where you genuinely understand the business.

The fee math is merciless - a 1.5% yearly edge for active fees needs persistent outperformance few deliver. Check any fund's expense ratio before admiring its past chart.

Where to start: a Nifty 50 ETF plus a gold ETF hedge covers most beginners' core; add single stocks only after finishing Track 2's ratio lessons.

Frequently asked questions

Can ETFs fall like stocks?

Yes - an index ETF mirrors its market, crashes included. Diversification softens single-stock risk, not market risk.
