What did Trump say about the Iran war?

On September 17, 2026, Trump said he hoped the war against Iran was near its end. "They want to make a deal. We'll see how that works out," he told reporters, adding he had heard from Iran "directly." The war began on February 28, 2026, when the US and Israel attacked Iran, and Tehran struck Israel and US bases in Gulf states.

The conflict has escalated in recent weeks, with Saudi aircraft pounding Yemen and Houthi fighters launching drones and missiles at Saudi cities. The extension of the war into Yemen threatens to worsen the global energy-supply shortage caused by the disruption of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, which carried about 20% of global oil and LNG before the conflict.

Oil prices have been volatile: Brent crude dropped $1.24 to $104.59/barrel on September 17 after reports of Saudi Arabia offering extra crude cargoes through Oman. Trump has claimed the war will end soon, but analysts remain sceptical.

What about the "biggest oil deal in history"?

On September 2, the White House announced that "President Trump Secures the Biggest Oil Deal in World History." Details remain sparse, but the deal appears to involve increased production from Gulf states to offset supply disruptions from the Iran war. The announcement came as oil prices remain above $100/barrel, squeezing consumers and businesses worldwide.

What did Trump say about 9/11 records?

On September 14, Trump said he would consider releasing 9/11 records after families of victims requested it. Several families have long alleged that a group of extremist religious leaders in Saudi Arabia gained influence in the Saudi government and aided the hijackers. Fifteen of the 19 hijackers were Saudis, but the Saudi government has denied any involvement.

"I'm going to look at it when I get back," Trump said during a trip to Ireland. The families have asked Trump to declassify and release the relevant records, which could shed light on Saudi Arabia's alleged role in the attacks.

What about the "Major's List" on drug trafficking?

On September 15, Trump delivered the "Major's List" to Congress, designating 23 countries responsible for sourcing and transporting illicit drugs into the United States. Four countries — Afghanistan, Bolivia, Burma and Colombia — were listed as having "failed demonstrably during the previous 12 months to make substantial efforts to adhere to their obligations under international counternarcotics agreements."

The announcement is part of the Trump Administration's fight against illicit drugs and cartels, which the White House says is "continuing to save American lives."

What did Trump say about Greenland?

Trump announced a deal with Denmark to bolster US military presence in Greenland, giving the US "permanent control over security" while keeping the territory in Denmark's hands. The deal bans non-NATO bases and limits Chinese and Russian influence in the Arctic. Trump called it a "historic deal" and a "huge win" for the United States.

Trump's statements on war, oil and trade directly impact commodity prices, defence stocks and currency markets. The Iran war has pushed oil above $100/barrel, benefiting energy producers but hurting consumers and airlines. Defence stocks have rallied on increased military spending. The Greenland deal has boosted Arctic mining and rare-earth stocks.
