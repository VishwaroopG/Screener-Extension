Bottom line: Wall Street hyperscalers—Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta—are committing over $200 billion annually to generative AI data centers, chips, and server clusters. The critical question for investors is when this hardware capex converts into software integration revenue for Indian IT giants.

The Two Phases of the AI Supercycle

Market cycles in enterprise technology traditionally unfold in two distinct waves:

  • Phase 1: Silicon & Infrastructure (Current): Dominated by Nvidia, TSMC, Broadcom, and server OEMs. Hyperscalers build raw compute infrastructure. Discretionary software consulting deals in banking and retail are temporarily delayed to fund hardware purchases.
  • Phase 2: Enterprise Integration & Legacy Modernisation (Emerging): Fortune 500 corporations cannot deploy proprietary AI models on fragmented legacy mainframes and obsolete databases. They require massive data engineering, ERP cloud migration, and security governance.

The Indian IT Beneficiaries

Tier-1 Indian IT powerhouses (TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Wipro) and specialized digital engineering firms (LTIMindtree, Persistent Systems) are signing multi-year enterprise AI orchestration mandates. As hyperscaler capex matures from infrastructure to enterprise rollout, Indian IT operating margins and revenue guidance will experience substantial tailwinds.
