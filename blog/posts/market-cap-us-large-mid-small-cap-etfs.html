Bottom line: market cap (share price × shares outstanding) decides what a US stock is: large-cap above ~$10B, mid-cap $2–10B, small-cap below ~$2B. Large-caps anchor, mid-caps blend, small-caps swing and each needs a different screening lens.

The three US size buckets

  • Large-cap (S&P 500): Apple, Microsoft, Exxon liquid, covered by 30 analysts, moves with the index. Start here for core holdings.
  • Mid-cap (S&P 400): the sweet spot many investors skip established businesses still growing, often takeover targets.
  • Small-cap (Russell 2000): highest dispersion, thinnest floats, biggest reaction to Fed moves. A 100 bps jump in the 10-year punishes back-ended small-cap cash flows most.

Best for: beginners who own five large-caps and wonder what diversification actually means.

Why size changes your screen

For large-caps, demand consistent ROCE, dividend coverage and reasonable P/E vs peers. For mid-caps, add revenue growth above 10% with leverage under 2x EBITDA. For small-caps, tighten further: net debt / EBITDA below 1.5x, operating cash flow above 70% of EBITDA, no big maturities in 12 months the same overlay analysts use when the US 10-year sits above 5%.

ETFs vs individual stocks by size

Large-cap exposure is cheap via S&P 500 ETFs; stock-picking edge is thin. Mid and small-caps are where single-stock research pays coverage is sparse and mispricing is common. A practical split: index the large-cap core, pick your spots in mid and small-caps where you can actually read every filing.

Best for: investors deciding between SPY, mid/small-cap ETFs, or hand-picked stocks.

Frequently asked questions

Are small-caps riskier?

Yes higher volatility, lower liquidity, greater rate sensitivity. Size positions accordingly.

What is the simplest US diversification?

An S&P 500 core plus a mid/small satellite. Rebalance yearly, not weekly.

Is this financial advice?

No. Educational explainer only. Always do your own research.
