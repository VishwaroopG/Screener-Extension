Bottom line: dividend yield (annual dividend / share price) tells you the cash return for holding a US stock. Around 1.5% is the S&P 500 norm — above ~4% is either a bargain or a trap. The difference is payout safety, and it takes 60 seconds to check.

How dividend yield works on NYSE and Nasdaq

A $100 stock paying $3 a year yields 3%. Prices move daily, so yield moves inversely: when Johnson & Johnson dips, its yield rises without the company doing anything. Nasdaq growth stocks often yield under 1% because they reinvest; NYSE staples, utilities and REITs yield more because they pay out. Neither is better — they are different jobs in a portfolio.

Best for: income investors comparing a dividend payer against a Treasury now yielding 5%+.

Yield trap vs yield opportunity

  • Trap signs: yield above ~7%, payout ratio above 90%, dividend frozen for years then suddenly raised, heavy debt maturing in 12 months.
  • Quality signs: payout ratio 30–60%, 10+ years of consecutive raises (Dividend Aristocrats), free cash flow covering the dividend 1.5x or more.
  • Sector norms: 4% is normal for a utility or REIT and alarming for a software stock — always compare peers first.

The 2026 twist: 5% risk-free competition

With the US 10-year above 5.10% and the Fed at 3.75–4.00%, a 2% yielder must offer real growth to compete with cash. That is why dividend growth — not headline yield — matters most right now. A 2.5% yielder compounding payouts at 8% a year beats a stagnant 5% yielder within a few years, with less risk of a cut.

Best for: anyone tempted to chase a double-digit yield in a high-rate market.

Frequently asked questions

Is a high dividend yield always good?

No. The market often prices a cut before it happens — a soaring yield can be a warning, not a gift.

Do Nasdaq stocks pay dividends?

Many large ones now do (Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia), but yields are small. Nasdaq is built for growth; NYSE staples and utilities carry income portfolios.

Is this financial advice?

No. This explains how yield works so you can screen income stocks better. Always do your own research.
