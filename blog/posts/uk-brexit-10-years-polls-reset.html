TL;DR: Ten years after the 2016 referendum, Brexit has become a "political and economic failure" in the eyes of most UK voters. Polls show 54% now say leaving the EU was the wrong decision, and 48–52% would vote to rejoin if asked today. The UK-EU relationship is being "reset" under new Prime Minister Andy Burnham, but friction remains — from trade barriers to Northern Ireland's border. For investors, the story matters because the UK market is deeply undervalued relative to US and European peers. What do polls say about Brexit now?

The numbers are striking. According to YouGov's Brexit tracker, 54% of UK voters say Brexit was the wrong decision — the widest margin since systematic tracking began. Only 36% say it was right. An Ipsos poll found that 52% would vote to rejoin the EU if a referendum were held tomorrow, versus 32–38% who would vote to stay out.

But the picture is nuanced. Only 11% of voters say EU membership should be a top political priority, and 52% don't want a second referendum. Many who think Brexit was a mistake are not enthusiastic about the disruption of rejoining.

Who still supports Brexit?

Support for Brexit is highest among over-65s (52%), non-degree voters in coastal and post-industrial England, and current Reform UK voters. Among 18–34s and degree-educated voters, fewer than a quarter still say Brexit was right. Among 2016 Leave voters, Reform UK now leads at 38–42%, followed by Conservatives at 28%.

What's the UK-EU "reset"?

Under new Prime Andy Burnham (who replaced Keir Starmer in July 2026), the UK has pursued closer ties with the EU. A May 2026 summit agreed to roll over EU fishermen's access to UK waters for 12 years and extended energy cooperation. Negotiations are also under way for a youth experience scheme allowing young people to live and work across borders.

But progress has been slow. The House of Lords reported that "virtually every estimate" of Brexit's economic cost is "at least 4% of GDP." The UK failed to regain access to the EU's Schengen Information System, which was used an estimated 600 million times a year by UK police before Brexit.

What about Northern Ireland?

Northern Ireland remains the most sensitive flashpoint. The Brexit border has created trade frictions between the province and Great Britain, with the EU's single-market rules creating a customs border in the Irish Sea. A June 2026 analysis called it "the UK's failed 10-year bid to fix the Brexit border."

What about EU citizens in the UK?

In April 2026, the Home Office began cracking down on post-Brexit residency rights for EU citizens. Officials are using travel data to identify those with "pre-settled status" who have been outside the UK for extended periods. Of the 6.2 million who applied for status after Brexit, 1.4 million remain on pre-settled status.

Why do investors care?

The UK stock market has been chronically undervalued relative to US and European peers. The FTSE 100 trades at roughly 12x forward earnings versus 20x+ for the S&P 500. Many investors see a potential UK-EU reset as a catalyst for re-rating — especially in financial services, industrials and consumer stocks that benefit from reduced trade friction.

But political uncertainty remains. Nigel Farage's Reform UK party is polling at 23–25%, and the debate over EU membership isn't going away. Investors need to watch both the political landscape and the economic data.

Frequently asked questions

Would the UK rejoin the EU?

Polls suggest 48–52% would vote to rejoin, but only 11% say it should be a top political priority. Rejoining would require a referendum, years of negotiations, and political will that doesn't currently exist.

How has Brexit affected the UK economy?

Most estimates put the cost at 4% of GDP. Trade friction with the EU has hurt exporters, reduced business investment, and created labour shortages in sectors that relied on EU workers.

What is the UK-EU "reset"?

Under PM Andy Burnham, the UK has pursued closer ties with the EU, including rolling over fishing access and extending energy cooperation. But progress has been slow, and major issues like security cooperation and single-market access remain unresolved.

Is the UK stock market undervalued?

The FTSE 100 trades at roughly 12x forward earnings versus 20x+ for the S&P 500. Many investors see a UK-EU reset as a potential catalyst for re-rating, though political uncertainty remains a headwind.
