Bottom line: US real GDP slowed to 1.5% in Q2 2026 (from 2.1%) while nominal GDP ran at 8.0% with inflation near 3.2%. The Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% and may go to 4.25%. Recession odds range from 35% (J.P. Morgan) to "unusually low" (RBC). For a large Indian equity mandate, the label matters less than the playbook. Here it is.

Base case: mid-to-late cycle, not recession — yet

Deutsche Bank expects US growth near 2% in both 2026 and 2027; RBC sees a mid-to-late cycle with low 12-month recession risk. AI capex — up to $1 trillion by the top hyperscalers in 2027 — plus large deficits keep nominal growth hot even as real growth cools. S&P earnings were among the strongest in five years. Translation: sticky inflation plus strong earnings plus elevated yields competing with equities — a stock-picker's regime. September proved it in India: the Nifty fell ~3.9% on foreign selling, cushioned by over ₹52,600 crore of domestic institutional buying.

Three scenarios, one dashboard

  • Soft landing (~55%): Fed near 4.25% caps inflation toward 3% in 2027 — foreign selling pauses, small-cap re-rating on lower discount rates.
  • Sticky slowdown (~30%): growth 1–1.5%, US 10-year above 5%, Brent near $90 — range-bound beta, primary market beats secondary, favour cash-generators.
  • Recession (15–35%): payrolls crack, AI capex pauses, US equities drop 15%+ — credit spreads widen, EM outflows, only funded order books and net-cash balance sheets survive.

The middle two scenarios carry ~85% combined weight — which is why the stance is: stay invested, but defensively positioned, not in cash.

The five rebalancing levers

1. Quality over beta — tighten entry screens

Net Debt/EBITDA below 1.5x, operating cash flow above 70% of EBITDA, ROCE above stressed cost of equity + 300 bps, funded order book only. In 2026 this single filter would have avoided most leveraged project SPVs and resin-price casualties.

2. Barbell duration

Pair short-duration cash compounders (distribution businesses with pricing power) with select long-duration capex winners commercialising within two quarters. Avoid mid-duration stories — capex done, cash flow three years out — they suffer most when discount rates rise.

3. Export versus domestic split

Keep 20–30% in certified US exporters (electronics assembly, harnesses, forgings) as a dollar hedge; rest in domestic infra and materials with visible award pipelines. If the rupee slides past 96, exporters cushion dollar returns.

4. Primary-market selectivity

Foreign investors bought over ₹5,500 crore of IPOs on September 24 while dumping secondaries. Mirror that selectivity: participate only where pricing leaves 20%+ upside on stressed discount rates, not scarcity premium.

5. Cash and a rebalance calendar

Hold 5–10% tactical cash in a slowdown regime; rebalance quarterly on KPI trackers (order inflow, receivable days, spread trends), not daily on Fed headlines. Add AI-value-chain exposure (energy, data centres, machinery) on 10%+ drawdowns rather than chasing peaks.

What would flip the stance

  • Bullish pivot: Fed pauses + US 10-year below 4.5% + Brent to $75–80 + stable rupee — add small-cap beta, extend duration, trim cash to 0–3%.
  • Bearish pivot: US payrolls break + S&P down 15% + Brent above $110 + outflows past ₹3 lakh crore — raise cash to 15%, cut all net-debt names, keep only net-cash companies.
  • Midterm wildcard (Nov 3, 2026): pre-vote volatility is normal and post-vote clarity usually supports equities — no aggressive pre-positioning either way.

The goal is not to predict Washington. It is to build a portfolio that survives any Washington outcome — and buys aggressively when others are forced to sell.
