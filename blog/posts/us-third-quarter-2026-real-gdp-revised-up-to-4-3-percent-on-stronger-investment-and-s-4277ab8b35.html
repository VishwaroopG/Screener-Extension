What Happened

Real gross domestic product increased at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 4.3 percent in the third quarter of 2026, according to the second estimate released by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis. This represents an upward revision of 0.9 percentage point from the advance estimate published earlier, following a 2.2 percent growth rate in the second quarter. The acceleration was primarily supported by stronger consumer spending and private domestic investment, alongside an upturn in government spending, which collectively outweighed a contraction in exports.

Private domestic purchasers experienced robust growth, with real final sales jumping 5.8 percent, also revised upward from earlier figures. On the inflation front, the price index for gross domestic purchases was revised down to a 2.7 percent increase. The personal consumption expenditures price index rose at an annual rate of 2.1 percent, while the core PCE price index excluding food and energy held steady at 2.5 percent. Corporate profits from current production increased by $206.0 billion in the third quarter, moderating from the $384.0 billion expansion seen in the second quarter.

Why It Matters

Revisions of this magnitude to GDP growth and price metrics are critical for assessing the underlying momentum of the world's largest economy. The upward shift in real GDP confirms that domestic demand remained remarkably resilient through the third quarter, powered largely by intellectual property products, software, and specific consumer retail categories including pharmaceuticals and recreational goods. At the same time, the downward revision to the gross domestic purchases price index alongside stable PCE inflation readings suggests that economic expansion is proceeding without a re-acceleration of headline inflationary pressures.

For financial markets, these growth and inflation dynamics provide vital input for monetary policy evaluation. Strong growth combined with contained core inflation metrics influences projections regarding interest rate paths and economic soft landing scenarios. Corporate earnings prospects also draw direct support from the ongoing growth in current-production profits, even as the quarter-over-quarter dollar increase slowed compared to the spring period.

What to Watch and Uncertainties

Market participants and economists will monitor upcoming data revisions and subsequent releases from the Bureau of Economic Analysis, including the third estimate scheduled for later in December, which will incorporate additional industry-level and profit details. Key variables to track include potential revisions to consumer health-care spending and retail trade data that initially drove diverging adjustments across goods and services sectors.

Conditional analysis points to ongoing uncertainty regarding how sustained domestic demand and business investment will interact with prospective monetary policy adjustments. Analysts will continue to evaluate incoming employment, retail, and inflation data to determine whether the robust third-quarter momentum can be sustained into the final months of the year and into subsequent quarters.
