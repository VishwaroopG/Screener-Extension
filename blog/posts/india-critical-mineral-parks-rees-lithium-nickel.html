India is moving to fix the weakest link in its clean-energy and electronics ambitions — not mining, but processing. The government has announced four dedicated critical mineral parks, each built around a different mineral ecosystem, to convert raw ores into high-value materials at home instead of shipping them abroad for refining.

Four states, four specialties

  • Andhra Pradesh — rare earth elements, titanium and zirconium (exact site still to be finalised).
  • Gujarat (Dahej, Bharuch) — a downstream hub for rare-earth permanent magnets, the components inside EV motors, wind turbines and electronics.
  • Maharashtra (near Dighi Port) — lithium and allied sectors, feeding the battery supply chain.
  • Odisha (Paradip) — nickel processing.

Three of the four states have already submitted detailed project reports, according to Anshoo Pandey, director of the National Critical Mineral Mission, who shared the details at a CII summit on advanced materials in New Delhi.

Why processing matters more than mining

India has deposits of several critical minerals, but it lacks enough separation and refining capacity. So even domestically mined material often travels overseas to be converted into usable inputs — leaving the country exposed to supply-chain shocks. Each park will bundle beneficiation, extraction, separation and processing in one place, creating end-to-end value chains from mineral to finished product.

The bigger push

The parks sit inside a wider strategy. The Centre has flagged 30 minerals as strategically critical, spanning EVs, batteries, renewables, defence and semiconductors. Alongside the parks, the government is accelerating exploration — the Geological Survey of India has taken up 300 critical mineral exploration projects this year alone — and hunting for overseas reserves. Mines Minister G Kishan Reddy said the mines ministry is working with the external affairs ministry to guide private companies chasing opportunities in mineral-rich countries, with dedicated mineral officers posted in embassies there.

His framing was blunt: the whole modern technology stack, from artificial intelligence to semiconductors, hangs on a single thread of critical minerals.

What investors should watch

Processing parks take years to build, so treat this as direction, not an immediate earnings trigger. But the direction is clear: domestic value addition in batteries, magnets, renewables and defence supply chains. Companies positioned across mining services, refining equipment, magnet manufacturing and battery materials stand to benefit as these ecosystems take shape — and reduced import dependence is a structural positive for India's current account over time.
