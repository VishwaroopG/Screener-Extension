Bottom line: when real yields top 2.5% and equities wobble, US investors reach for gold - but gold ETFs (GLD, IAU, SGOL) and gold miners (Newmont, Barrick, GDX) are different trades. ETFs track the metal; miners are leveraged equities with costs, strikes and politics attached.

ETF vs miners in 30 seconds

  • Gold ETFs: hold bullion or futures, charge ~0.10–0.40% expense ratios, move with spot gold minus fees. Calm, no operational risk, no dividend to speak of.
  • Miners: operating leverage cuts both ways - a 10% gold rise can lift miner earnings 30%+, but energy, labour and royalty costs bite when Brent sits above $100. Expect 1.5–2x gold's volatility.
  • Royalty names: Franco-Nevada and Wheaton sit between the two - diversified streams, fewer mine-level shocks.

Best for: risk-off allocators deciding whether they want insurance (ETF) or a trade (miners).

The October 2026 backdrop

Fed at 3.75–4.00%, 10-year above 5.10%, Brent above $100, dollar near rupee-96 equivalents - classic late-cycle cross-currents where gold historically holds bids while equities re-rate. Central-bank buying stays structural, but high real yields cap upside: gold pays no coupon, so every uptick in real rates raises its opportunity cost. Size accordingly - insurance, not all-in.

Quick screen before you buy

  • ETFs: compare expense ratio, spread and AUM (GLD vs IAU vs SGOL); check whether you want spot or futures exposure.
  • Miners: all-in sustaining cost (AISC) vs spot, net debt / EBITDA, reserve life, jurisdiction mix. A miner with $1,400 AISC at $2,600 gold has a very different margin of safety than one at $1,900.
  • Positioning: cap gold exposure, pair miners with an alert rather than a prayer - volatility demands exits planned in advance.

Frequently asked questions

Which is safer - GLD or GDX?

GLD-style bullion ETFs. GDX is an equity basket and behaves like one in sell-offs.

Do miners pay dividends?

Large ones often do, but payouts flex with gold prices - never underwrite a miner on yield alone.
