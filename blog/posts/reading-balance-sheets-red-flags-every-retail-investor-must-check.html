Bottom line: Income statements can be dressed up with aggressive revenue recognition and accounting gimmicks, but the balance sheet rarely lies. Screening for these five warning signs will protect you from catastrophic capital losses.

5 Deadly Financial Red Flags

  1. Surging Promoter Pledges: When company promoters borrow personal loans against pledged shares, a sharp decline in stock price can trigger forced institutional liquidation, creating a free fall.
  2. Divergence Between Net Profit and Operating Cash Flow: If reported net profit doubles while cash flow from operations stagnates or turns negative, profits exist only on paper as unpaid receivables.
  3. Ballooning Trade Receivables (Days Sales Outstanding Spike): Rapidly increasing receivables relative to sales suggests the company is stuffing channels or dealing with delinquent customers.
  4. Aggressive Short-Term Debt for Long-Term Assets: Funding long-duration capital expenditure with short-term commercial paper creates extreme refinancing risk when credit markets tighten.
  5. Frequent Auditor Resignations & Material Weaknesses: Mid-term auditor resignations citing lack of information or disagreements over valuation are an immediate signal to sell first and ask questions later.
