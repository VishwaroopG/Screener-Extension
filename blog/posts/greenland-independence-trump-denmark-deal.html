What happened with Greenland and Trump?

Since returning to the White House in January 2025, President Donald Trump has repeatedly said the US "needs" Greenland for national security reasons. He pointed to Russian and Chinese ships in Arctic waters and called the island — which is part of the Kingdom of Denmark — a "core national security interest." At the Davos World Economic Forum in January 2026, Trump called Greenland "enormous unsecured island" that is "actually part of North America" and "therefore a core national security interest."

Trump refused to rule out using military force to acquire the island, triggering an unprecedented diplomatic crisis between the US and Denmark — both founding members of NATO. Thousands protested across Denmark in January 2026, chanting "Greenland is not for sale" and marching toward the US embassy.

How did Europe respond?

Six major European nations — France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Spain and the United Kingdom — issued a joint statement with Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen, declaring that "Greenland belongs to its people" and that "only Denmark and Greenland can decide on matters concerning Denmark and Greenland." EU Commission President Ursula von der Leyen visited Nuuk in September 2026 and pledged €200 million in new investments for Greenland, focusing on connectivity, clean energy and critical raw materials.

What deal was reached?

In September 2026, Trump announced a deal with Denmark to bolster US military presence in Greenland. The agreement gives the US "permanent control over security" in Greenland while keeping the territory in Denmark's hands. Key provisions include:

  • No non-NATO bases — the deal bans any non-NATO military installation in Greenland
  • No Chinese or Russian bases — adversaries cannot establish military presence on the island
  • Investment restrictions — limits on adversary investments in Greenland
  • Sovereignty preserved — the agreement "recognizes the sovereignty and territorial integrity" of Greenland and Denmark

Danish PM Frederiksen said the deal "recognizes Greenland's interests" and was signed by all three governments during the UN General Assembly.

What about Greenland's independence movement?

Trump's pursuit has actually strengthened Greenland's independence movement. In March 2026, the pro-independence Naleraq party won its first seat in the Danish parliament. The party advocates swift independence from Denmark, though all five Greenlandic parties ultimately support eventual independence.

A 2025 poll showed 84% of Greenlanders support independence, though 61% opposed it if it meant a lower standard of living. Greenland relies on Denmark for about $500 million in annual block grants and much of its administration.

Why do investors care about Greenland?

Greenland sits on vast deposits of rare earth minerals — the building blocks of defence systems, EV motors, wind turbines and electronics. As the US, China and Europe compete for control of these supply chains, Greenland's mineral wealth becomes a strategic asset. Companies positioned across Arctic mining, rare earth processing, defence contracting and clean energy stand to benefit from the geopolitical competition.

The deal also reduces uncertainty about Greenland's future, potentially accelerating investment in the island's mineral sector.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Trump want Greenland?

Trump cites national security, pointing to Russian and Chinese Arctic activity. Greenland's strategic location and rare earth mineral deposits make it valuable for US defence and supply-chain security.

Did the US buy Greenland?

No. The September 2026 deal allows expanded US military presence while keeping Greenland under Danish sovereignty. Trump described it as "permanent control over security" rather than a purchase.

Will Greenland become independent?

All major Greenlandic parties support eventual independence, but the timeline is uncertain. The 2009 Self-Government Act gives Greenland the right to hold a referendum, but economic dependence on Denmark complicates the path.

What rare earth minerals does Greenland have?

Greenland holds deposits of rare earth elements, uranium, zinc, iron ore and other critical minerals. The Kvanefjeld project contains one of the world's largest rare earth deposits, though mining is politically controversial due to environmental concerns.
