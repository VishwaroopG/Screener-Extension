Bottom line: in March 2026 phenol hit Rs 160/kg (from Rs 85), formaldehyde crossed Rs 35 (from Rs 14) and melamine hit Rs 135 (from Rs 88) — then moderated to Rs 120 / Rs 23–24 / Rs 100 after shipping lanes reopened in June. If you follow laminates, plywood or panels, that swing is the earnings revision. Here is how a Building Materials analyst models it.

What happened in 2026 — a textbook shock

The West Asia conflict tightened the Strait of Hormuz in Q1 2026. Benzene and propylene — the feedstocks for phenol — spiked, freight seized up, and India (which consumes about 650 KT of phenol a year, growing 8–9% annually) felt it instantly. Some panel makers contemplated halting production; the industry took price hikes just to reach breakeven. Relief came only when lanes reopened plus a temporary customs-duty exemption on phenol (April–June 2026). Prices softened — but not back to start. That asymmetry is the whole margin story.

The chain: Brent to benzene to phenol to resin to laminate EBITDA

Brent above $100 does not just hurt oil marketers. Cumene becomes phenol plus acetone, which becomes phenolic resin, which becomes plywood adhesive and laminate. A $10/barrel Brent move typically reaches benzene in 3–5 weeks, then phenol in 4–8 weeks. Laminate makers hold 30–45 days of resin inventory, so a Q1 cost shock hits profits in Q2 — which is why earnings downgrades cluster one quarter after the commodity headline.

Analyst rule: track three spreads weekly — phenol-minus-benzene, resin-minus-phenol, laminate realisation-minus-resin. When the third compresses two weeks in a row, cut next-quarter EBITDA 100–150 bps pre-emptively.

Why price hikes never fully offset

  • Pass-through is capped: dealers accept 3–5% quarterly hikes; a 70–90% input spike needs 12–15% realisation. Volumes crack first.
  • Mix shifts down: in stress quarters buyers trade down from premium laminates to economy boards, hurting the high-margin tail.
  • Working capital balloons: costlier inventory plus slower receivables can drag cash conversion from ~70% of EBITDA to 40–50%.

A sensitivity tree for a laminate maker

Base EBITDA margin 12%. Shock case (phenol +50%, formaldehyde +100%, only a 4% price hike): resin cost per kg up ~35%, gross margin down ~280 bps, EBITDA down ~220 bps after partial pass-through. Relief case (phenol down 25% from peak, hikes retained): margin up ~180 bps — this inflection is what longs pay for. New plants amplify both directions through operating leverage.

What to watch now

  1. Brent $90 vs $100: forecasts see ~$90 in Q4 and $75–80 in 2027 if calm holds. Sustained $100+ re-tests the March highs.
  2. Benzene and propylene weekly: they lead phenol by about a month — Q2 softening drove the June relief.
  3. Chinese plant run-rates (low-80s %): high runs mean ample supply and cap the upside; new Chinese capacity is the structural lid on 2026–27 prices.
  4. New domestic phenol capacity in India: reduces import dependence structurally — the base case for price stability in FY26.

In Building Materials, macro is micro. Model commodities as explicitly as volumes, demand proof of pass-through before re-rating, and buy margin troughs only when spreads turn — not when headlines calm.
