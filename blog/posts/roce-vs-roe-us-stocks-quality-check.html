Bottom line: ROE tells you how hard shareholder money is working. ROCE tells you how hard all capital - equity plus debt - is working. For US stocks, a high ROE funded by debt is a red flag, while a high ROCE is usually real quality. Check both before you trust a P/E.

ROE vs ROCE in 30 seconds

ROE = Net Income / Shareholder Equity. Apple or Microsoft can post 30%+ ROE partly because buybacks shrink equity. ROCE = EBIT / Capital Employed (equity + debt). It cannot be gamed the same way - a company borrowing heavily to juice ROE will show a weaker ROCE. That is why Ticker Screener's AI verdict uses P/E plus ROCE, not P/E alone.

Best for: anyone screening S&P 500 stocks who wants one quality filter that survives buybacks and leverage.

What is a good number for US stocks?

  • ROCE above ~15% sustained for 5 years is quality territory for most US sectors; above 20% is elite (software, payments, wide-moat consumer).
  • ROE above ~15% is solid, but always compare against ROCE - if ROE is 35% and ROCE is 9%, leverage is doing the work.
  • Capital-light vs capital-heavy: a railroad or utility at 10% ROCE can be excellent; a SaaS company at 10% ROCE is mediocre. Compare peers, never the whole market.

Three traps ROE hides

  • Buyback illusion: big repurchases shrink equity and inflate ROE even when profits are flat. ROCE stays honest.
  • Bank leverage: US banks routinely show 10–12% ROE on 10x leverage - ROCE and return on assets tell the real story.
  • Cyclical peaks: miners, airlines and homebuilders post record ROE at the top of the cycle, just before mean reversion. Check 5-year average ROCE instead.

Best for: value investors burned by a "cheap high-ROE" stock that turned out to be a leverage story.

Frequently asked questions

Is a high ROE always good?

No. Check debt-to-equity alongside it. High ROE with high debt is fragile; high ROE with high ROCE is quality.

Which matters more for tech stocks?

ROCE, because buybacks are huge in big tech. A 30% ROE means little if ROCE is drifting down.

Is this financial advice?

No. ROE and ROCE are screening lenses, not buy signals. Always do your own research.
