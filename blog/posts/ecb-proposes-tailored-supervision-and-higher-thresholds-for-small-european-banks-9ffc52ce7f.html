What happened

In a published contribution by Frank Elderson, Member of the Executive Board and Vice-Chair of the Supervisory Board of the European Central Bank, the central bank detailed a series of practical proposals to reform the oversight of smaller, non-complex banks across the euro area. The initiative seeks to introduce a more proportionate regulatory approach that reflects the specific business models, risk profiles, and simpler structures of regional institutions compared to large international banking groups.

Key proposals include raising the balance sheet threshold for qualifying as a small and non-complex bank from five billion euros to ten billion euros, subject to national authority discretion and domestic banking structures. Furthermore, the proposals aim to redefine technical non-complex criteria tied to resolution planning, potentially bringing an estimated 150 additional institutions under the proportionate framework. Supervisory modifications also target reporting obligations, with ongoing reforms alongside the European Banking Authority intended to reduce small bank data reporting requirements to a fraction of those mandated for large counterparts.

Why it matters

Small and regional banks form a critical pillar of credit creation, financing the real economy, local businesses, and households across Europe. Regulatory frameworks originally designed for globally systemic institutions can impose disproportionate administrative and financial compliance costs on simpler lenders. By lowering compliance burdens and streamlining routine supervisory stress-test exercises, regulatory authorities aim to free up resources for smaller institutions.

These operational efficiencies can enhance the cost-to-income ratios of regional banks, enabling them to fund necessary investments in digitalisation, cybersecurity, and operational resilience. At the same time, the central bank emphasised that reduced complexity and tailored oversight must not compromise rigorous safety standards, ensuring that retail depositors maintain full confidence across all categories of regulated financial institutions.

European context and risk factors

The regulatory adjustments carry distinct regional relevance, particularly for jurisdictions like Germany where a high concentration of regional and cooperative banks serves the domestic Mittelstand. Consolidation trends among smaller institutions have naturally increased average balance sheet sizes without necessarily escalating underlying business complexity or risk profiles.

Despite the push for proportionality, supervisors noted that smaller institutions remain exposed to broader macroeconomic and systemic vulnerabilities. Key ongoing risks include rising cyber threats, macroeconomic uncertainties linked to geopolitical tensions, rapid digital transformation, and the increasing financial impact of climate and environmental factors on household and corporate borrowers.

What to watch

Investors and financial analysts will monitor the formal legislative progress required to amend the existing prudential frameworks, particularly the formal adoption of higher asset thresholds by regulatory bodies. Implementation timelines for streamlined reporting mandates and centralized stress-testing projections will also serve as key indicators of the pace of supervisory reform.

Conditional analyses suggest that while capital relief or operational savings could improve institutional earnings efficiency for regional lenders, broader macroeconomic conditions, localized credit demand, and deposit competition will ultimately dictate sector performance. Future updates from the European Central Bank and national competent authorities will clarify how discretionary threshold increases are applied across different member states.
