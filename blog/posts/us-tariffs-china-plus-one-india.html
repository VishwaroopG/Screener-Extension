Bottom line: in February 2026 the US–India reciprocal tariff was cut to 18% from 25% (with the extra Russia-oil surcharge removed), while Chinese electronics still face 25–145% depending on classification — and roughly 60% of US smartphone imports now come from India versus 6% in late 2024. Tariffs are no longer headlines; they are earnings drivers. Here is how to separate real China+1 beneficiaries from story stocks.

What actually changed in 2025–26

  • US–India deal (Feb 2026): reciprocal tariff 25% → 18%, extra 25% Russia-oil tariff removed, India to cut tariffs on US industrial goods. Textiles, leather and jewellery exporters got immediate relief.
  • China still penalised: Section 301 and related probes keep Chinese electronics at 25–145%. At 25%+ tariffs, India's total landed cost is roughly 31% cheaper on a representative assembly.
  • Proof of shift: Apple moved about $2 billion of iPhones from India in March 2025; by May 2025 two-thirds of US smartphone imports were India-origin, still ~60% through mid-2026. Clothing did not shift — because India's apparel tariffs ended up higher than China's. Product-level gaps decide everything.

Analyst lesson: never say "China+1 benefits India." Ask what the product-level tariff gap between China and India is for that exact goods code, and whether the Indian supplier has certified capacity today.

A 4-box China+1 filter

  • Tariff gap: India at least ~15% cheaper than China, durable for 3–5 years. Flat 10–12.5% tariffs give no reason to move.
  • Certified capacity: IPC-A-610 / IATF / AS9100 certification plus existing US customer references — not MOUs and press releases.
  • Domestic value-add: rising local sourcing, not screwdriver assembly (phone assembly value-add in India is still under 20% versus over 40% in China).
  • Customer stickiness: multi-year RFQs and dual qualification — spot orders reverse, as seen when some iPhone orders moved back to China ahead of a launch.

Where India is genuinely ready (2026)

High conviction: EMS, wire harnesses, forgings and castings, precision machining

Foxconn and Tata Electronics for assembly; Motherson and Minda for harnesses; Rajkot–Ludhiana–Pune for forgings; Pune–Coimbatore–Bengaluru for machining. Tariff exposure above 25%, export-mature, qualification in 60 days to 6 months.

Selective: auto components, specialty chemicals, technical textiles

Winners where US buyers need friend-shoring for compliance. But India's trade deficit with China widened past $112 billion in FY25–26 — component depth is still thin. Model only companies localising their bill of materials, not pure assemblers.

Avoid for now: full-stack semiconductors, cosmetic-grade plastics

OSAT packaging is live in Gujarat; a full fab is a 2027+ story. Do not capitalise 2028 earnings into a 2026 target price.

How it flows into valuation

  • Revenue: only qualified RFQs in the base case; pipeline orders get 30–50% probability weights, not 100%.
  • Margins: year-one China+1 margins run 200–300 bps below steady state (overtime, rework, expedited freight). Normalise from year two.
  • Working capital: 8–12 weeks of safety stock during dual running — penalise via higher debt, not just lower EBIT.
  • Multiples: no China+1 premium multiple until export revenue exceeds 25% of sales for two straight quarters.

The 18% deal makes India the least-bad alternative, not an automatic winner: if new tariff rounds do not keep a wide China-versus-India gap, supply chains drift back to China on cost. Overweight certified exporters with passed US customer audits; underweight domestic-only names trading on China+1 narratives.
